Bio

I'm a physics PhD student at the University of Cambridge, where I build multiphysics simulations of contrail formation and evolution. Contrails may account for a large share of aviation's warming effect, and understanding how they form is the first step towards avoiding them. My PhD is part of the Cambridge–Boeing collaboration and is funded by Boeing, alongside a PhD scholarship from the German Academic Scholarship Foundation (Studienstiftung).

I studied physics at UCL, focusing on condensed matter in my master's year. Along the way I found I enjoyed the computing side most, so I moved to scientific computing for my MPhil at Cambridge. Since then my work has been in numerical methods and high-performance computing, applied to problems in aviation and climate.

Outside research I supervise Cambridge undergraduates and tutor, and I've organised speaker events and a college research conference. I also captained the Clare Hall Boat Club. I speak English and German fluently, and some Greek.

Current work

PhD research

Multiphysics modelling of contrails

Simulating the exhaust jet behind an aircraft engine, from the nozzle through the mixing that leads to ice formation. I work with Nikos Nikiforakis's group in Cambridge and present the work at the annual Cambridge–Boeing review.

Paper in preparation

Particle-laden jets

A numerical model for jets carrying soot, sulfate and water-droplet particles, validated step by step from plane jets to round jets. A follow-up will add an ice model to capture contrail formation.

Collaboration

Lab contrails, simulated one-to-one

Working with a University of Toronto group whose cold-chamber facility produces contrails in the lab, so we can compare simulation and experiment directly.

Side project

No Contrail Guide

This website. It helps travellers choose departure times and aircraft that keep their flight's contrails out of the night sky. Try the guide.
